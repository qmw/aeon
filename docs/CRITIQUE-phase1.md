# Phase 1 critic findings — NOT yet fixed. Treat as the backlog.

## critique 1 — 36/100
This is a Unity hex-terrain prototype with a nice noise function, not a shipping 4X world. Put it beside a Civ VI screenshot at this zoom and Civ wins instantly and for boring reasons: Civ has a light direction you can name, cast shadows that tell you the mountains are tall, water that reads as depth, and a coast silhouette that looks authored. This has a 47–217 luminance range with snow at 217 and lit grass at 210 — the peaks are the same brightness as the fields — and I measured the terrain under the massif (141,153,117) as brighter than nothing at all versus open lit grass (149,164,113), meaning a multi-thousand-metre range casts literally zero shadow. Macro-wise the "range" is one white blob dropped in a plain with grass touching snow directly, and the rivers are painted polylines that die mid-tile. Hard reject.

**Problems**
- Grading is dead. Full-frame luminance sits between 47 and 217 — no blacks, no highlights, nothing clipped in either direction, mean 147 / std 36. Snow peaks (217) and lit grassland (210) are 7 levels apart, so the single most important silhouette element in the frame has no tonal separation from the ground it sits on.
- No cast shadows at all. Sampled terrain in the lee of the central massif reads (141,153,117); open lit grass reads (149,164,113) — indistinguishable. No CSM, no contact shadows, no terrain AO. Every hex is lit as if by a uniform dome, which is why the whole plate looks like a papier-mache diorama.
- The mountains are a blob, not a range. One roughly circular cluster of white cones parked in the middle of a plain, with no arc, no ridgeline continuity, no foothill ramp, no erosion signature, no relationship to a plate margin or coastline, and no rain shadow behind it. Civ VI ranges read as authored geological spines; this reads as "height > threshold, paint white".
- Biome stack is a two-state switch. Grass abuts snow with zero intermediate bands — no exposed rock, no scree/talus, no alpine tundra, no treeline. Snow also coats every face equally including near-vertical ones, so it reads as a white plastic dip-coat rather than accumulation on upward-facing slopes.
- Rivers are decals, not a drainage network. Uniform width everywhere regardless of position in the basin, no confluences or Strahler ordering, multiple segments terminate mid-tile in open grassland, nothing visibly sources from the massif, and no delta or estuary where they meet the sea. No channel incision either — the heightfield is unaffected, so they float on top of the terrain.
- Zero atmospheric depth, and it is measurably inverted: far land at top-right (148,164,114, lum std 27.1) has HIGHER local contrast than near land at bottom-left (157,165,116, lum std 23.6). Nothing recedes. Compounding it, the top-right corner is raw gray viewport clear (113,115,116) with a hard straight diagonal where the world plane ends — programmer-art framing with no sky, horizon, cloud layer, or edge treatment.
- Water is a stepped shallow-to-deep colour lerp, not absorption. Concentric banding rings are visible in the transition zone, there is no Fresnel gradient toward the horizon (near and far water sit at the same value), and the shoreline foam is a constant-width, constant-brightness ring that ignores exposure, bathymetry and wave direction. The ocean specular is one noise tile with visible repetition top-right.
- Surfaces are flat matte at a single roughness, and the hex tessellation is showing. Tile-edge facets catch light at different angles and quilt the terrain into visibly separate plates (worst in the tan mid-left region), biome borders are hard per-tile fills, foliage is one identical blob mesh at one scale on a jitter grid with crunchy aliased silhouettes, and the snow is per-pixel dither noise that will crawl violently the moment the camera moves.

**Fixes**
- Replace thresholded fBm landmass generation with a tectonic pass: 8–12 Voronoi plates with assigned motion vectors, convergent boundaries producing arcuate mountain belts, divergent producing rifts and ocean ridges. This is what buys you directional coastlines, capes/bays hierarchy and ranges that correlate with a margin instead of blobs.
- Rebuild the massif as a cordillera: 15–25 hexes long, 3–5 wide, asymmetric cross-section (steep on the convergent side, ramped on the other), then run hydraulic erosion — 50+ iterations, talus repose angle ~35° — so ridgelines sharpen and valleys cut V/U profiles. Add a 4–6 hex foothill gradient before the snowline.
- Insert at least three intermediate altitude bands between forest and snow: forest → treeline shrub → alpine tundra → scree/talus → bare rock → snow. Mask snow by slope (dot(N,up) > 0.6) with a prevailing-wind lee bias so windward faces expose rock and lee faces hold drift. Never let grass albedo touch snow albedo in a single hex step.
- Regenerate rivers from flow accumulation: priority-flood fill the heightfield, D8 (or D-infinity) accumulate, threshold on discharge, then scale channel mesh width by sqrt(discharge) — roughly 0.3 hex-widths at Strahler order 1 up to 1.2 at order 5. Enforce termination at ocean or an endorheic lake so no segment dies mid-tile, carve the heightfield 0.5–1.5m along the channel so banks read, and stamp a wet-soil/riparian vegetation band one tile either side.
- Fix the tonemap and shadows together. Move to ACES (or equivalent filmic) with an S-curve, push sunlit snow to 245–255 with a specular sheen term, and let occluded valley floors and under-canopy reach 25–35 so the histogram actually spans the range instead of living at 47–217. Then add 4-cascade CSM (first cascade ~30m, PCF 5×5 or PCSS) — the massif should throw a 3–6 hex shadow — plus baked HBAO into a terrain texture channel for tile-junction and canopy contact darkening.
- Add aerial perspective: exponential height fog at density ~0.0015/unit tinted to the sky colour, plus a 0.35 desaturation term and a slight blue shift reaching maximum at far plane. Target roughly 40% contrast reduction on the far coastline relative to foreground — right now far terrain measures higher contrast than near, which is backwards.
- Replace the shallow/deep colour lerp with Beer-Lambert absorption on water depth (extinction ~[0.45, 0.12, 0.05] per metre for R/G/B) — that alone eliminates the concentric depth banding. Layer Schlick Fresnel at F0 = 0.02 with a sky cubemap reflection so horizon-facing water brightens and near water goes deep. Drive foam from a distance-to-shore SDF feathered over 0.5–2 tiles and modulate its intensity by shoreline gradient and wave-direction dot product instead of a constant ring.
- Add triplanar detail-normal at 2m tiling plus a second macro-normal at 12m to break the flat matte surfaces, and author a real roughness map instead of one global value: grass 0.85, wet sand at the waterline 0.45, snow 0.55 with a sheen lobe, rock 0.7. Current uniform roughness is what makes the whole plate read as modelling clay.
- Kill the hex quilting: average vertex normals across shared tile edges so the surface shades continuously, and render the hex outline as a subtle shader overlay rather than a geometric crease. Blend biome albedo across a 1-tile falloff using a noise-perturbed mask (domain-warped, ~3–5 hex warp scale) so borders stop reading as a spreadsheet fill.
- Add TAA — or at minimum MSAA 4× with alpha-to-coverage on foliage cards — and move the snow speckle from per-pixel noise into a mip-mapped world-space texture, otherwise it will crawl and shimmer in motion. While in there, give foliage 3–4 mesh variants with ±30% scale jitter and ±15° yaw, place via Poisson-disk with density driven by the moisture map, and add a contact-shadow decal under each canopy cluster. Finally, extend the ocean plane past the frustum or add a sky dome with a soft stylized world-edge falloff so the top-right corner stops showing raw gray clear colour behind a hard diagonal cut.

- Delete the whitecap dither entirely. Derive foam from the wave Jacobian/crest mask (saturate((cos_thresh - dot(N,up)) * 6)), advect it along a flowmap, and modulate with 3-octave FBM at 6m tiling; contrast-remap so crests are sparse and directional rather than uniform static.
- Depth-based water colour: sample scene depth, lerp shallow (0.42,0.74,0.72) to deep (0.02,0.13,0.24) on exp(-depth*0.18); offset the opaque buffer by N.xz*0.02*saturate(depth) for refraction and project animated caustics onto the seabed above 6m depth.
- Shoreline: replace the painted blobs with a depth-driven foam band, width = 1 - saturate(depth/0.8), animated on a 1.2s run-up/retreat sawtooth with a soft alpha ramp; multiply sand albedo by 0.75 and drop roughness to 0.25 in a 1.5-tile wet band; jitter the shore edge by 0.4 tiles so it stops reading as a mesh cut.
- Rivers: rebuild as spline-swept meshes with width 0.4-1.2 tiles driven by upstream flow accumulation, carve the heightmap 0.6-1.2m with a 2m bank falloff, blend the terrain splat to gravel/mud on the banks, shade with the same depth/refraction water shader plus a flowmap-scrolled normal at 1.5m tiling, and spawn foam where bed slope exceeds 8 degrees.
- Lakes: apply the ocean water material with 3m-tile normals, a planar reflection or box-projected cubemap (SSR at minimum), a depth ramp into dark teal, and inset the surface mesh so the shore blends over ~0.5 tiles of vertex alpha. A lake must never be a flat unlit hex fill.
- Regrade: ACES tonemap with the foreground reaching 0.02 black and 0.98 white, and apply fog strictly as a function of distance so contrast falls off with depth instead of a global milk layer. Add a directional light with a 3-4 cascade shadowmap — there is currently no cast shadow on the water or from any coastal cliff.
- Fix the corner artifact: clamp the refraction/chromatic-aberration sample to the buffer bounds and mask CA to 0 in the outer 3% of the frame; enable TAA (or 4x MSAA with alpha-to-coverage) on the water silhouette to kill the shoreline jaggies.
- Strip the global per-pixel grain/dither overlay being used as fake detail and replace it with a triplanar detail-normal at 2m tiling and 0.35 intensity plus proper mip-biased albedo; that stipple is the single biggest reason the terrain and the water both read as programmer art.
