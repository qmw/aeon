## critique 1 — 52/100

It is deterministic, it does NOT slow down (turn cost fell 0.48x while entities grew 3.22x), and games actually conclude — conquest victory at T273/T351 with 23-31 captures and the full tech tree finished. tools/sim.mjs is an honest harness that asserts real invariants and exits 1. BUT: nothing in input.js or hud.js ever calls commandTo/orderMove/foundHere/fortify/setProduction/setResearch/buyProduction. Every player verb is unreachable from the app. endTurn() runs aiTurn for civs 1-3 only, so the human civ's units never move for the entire game — sim.mjs passes only because line 28 substitutes an AI for the missing input layer. ## critique 4 — 62/100
The simulation is real and genuinely deep — but the game is unplayable and the sim that "proves" it is a rubber stamp.

WHAT ACTUALLY WORKS (my own probe, 150-400 turns x 10 seeds, all four seats AI-driven, zero violations): yields finite and non-negative across 400 turns; cities grow 4->189/324 pop and complete all 20 building types; 86 tech gains verified at the moment of acquisition, never once out of prereq order; ~2,400 A* paths validated step-by-step — every step adjacent, never crossing impassable or element-illegal terrain, mountains provably unreachable; combat resolves (424-587 attacks, 150-190 unit deaths, 2-5 city captures, civs eliminated); fog is per-civ with distinct buffers and zero orders/attacks on never-seen tiles; turn loop is SUB-linear (3.5ms -> ~11-15ms while entities grew 5x, 400 turns in 3.4s); deterministic; survives 10x10 through 80x60 maps; Science/Culture/Domination victories all fire organically. turn.js is a real 4X engine (ZOC, embarkation tech-gating, flanking, siege, promotions, district adjacency, culture borders, truces, upkeep-driven disbanding). ai.js is a real opponent with fog-honest memory. WHY IT FAILS ANYWAY — two auto-fail conditions from the brief:

1. THE PLAYER CANNOT PLAY. Nothing in the entire repo calls game.commandTo(). Nothing calls game.action(). hud.js act() handles fortify/found/buy directly and for everything else dispatches CustomEvent('aeon:action') — and there is exactly ONE occurrence of that string in the whole codebase, the dispatch. No listener exists. Move, Attack, Build, Produce, Manage, Defend, Bombard are dead buttons that toggle a CSS `.on` class so they LOOK like they responded. The player can end turn, fortify, found, and purchase. That's it. state.mode, cycleProduction, entrench, and player cityStrike are unreachable code.

It prints "sim OK" when every attack deals 0 damage to defenders. It prints "sim OK" with city growth fully disabled, because `pop1 > pop0` is satisfied by FOUNDING cities, not growing them. Its header claims the human seat is "driven ONLY through the public verbs the UI is wired to" — false; it drives commandTo and action(), which the UI never calls. That false claim is precisely why defect #1 shipped. Its peace test is synthetic (hand-cranks state.turn), proving the function not the loop.

Also: fog of war is invisible in the shipped game — src/render/fx.js does not exist (main.js logs "[opt] skip", passes fx=null), units.js contains zero occurrences of "vis", and pushRender adds every civ's units and cities unconditionally. The AI is honestly fog-limited; the human is omniscient. And A* runs on ground truth — 2,085 of 21,928 path tiles (9.5%) lie in terrain the moving civ has never explored.

No simulation invariant was violated. But a 4X where you cannot move a unit, whose fog is unrendered, and whose test suite green-lights an empty AI is an engine with a diorama bolted on, not a working game.
